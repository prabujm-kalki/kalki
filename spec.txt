 Kalki BOS Notification, Reminder &amp; Escalation Engine A-to-Z Architecture &amp; Implementation Specification v1.0 Status: AUTHORITATIVE DESIGN BASELINE Purpose: Define a reliable, auditable, server-side engine for time-bound work, reminders, acknowledgement, overdue handling and escalation across Kalki BOS. Scope: Shared cross-system capability. Not a top-level business module. It supports People, Purchasing, Attendance, Payroll, Inventory, Sales, CRM, Finance, Command Center and future modules. Delivery strategy: Web/PWA first; Android APK later using the same backend contracts. Notification delivery is a channel; business timing and escalation logic remain server-side. 1. Executive Decision Kalki BOS shall implement a centralized Notification, Reminder &amp; Escalation Engine as a shared platform capability. The engine shall not depend on a browser tab being open. Time-bound work shall be evaluated server-side and shall produce durable work state, notification attempts, acknowledgement state, escalation state and audit evidence. Kalki BOS shall engineer for high reliability, but shall not claim literal 100% physical notification delivery. Phones can be powered off, disconnected, have notification permissions disabled, or be subject to operating-system restrictions. Reliability therefore comes from durable server-side scheduling, idempotent processing, retries, delivery/acknowledgement tracking, escalation, in-app visibility and audit. 1.1 Core principle No critical time-bound work may depend solely on the employee opening the browser. Every critical task must have: • server-side due-time monitoring • defined reminder/escalation stages • notification attempt tracking • acknowledgement/completion tracking • escalation when required • durable audit evidence • recovery after server/process interruption 1.2 Architectural placement Business Module ↓ Work / Situation / Task definition ↓ Work Instance ↓ Reminder &amp; Escalation Rules ↓ Server-side Scheduler / Due Engine ↓ Notification Orchestrator ↓ Delivery Channels ├── Web/PWA Push ├── In-app └── Future Android Push / WhatsApp / SMS ↓ Delivery + Acknowledgement + Escalation + Audit 2. Goals and Non-Goals 2.1 Goals Make time-bound operational work visible and actionable. Prevent silent failure when an employee does not open the web application. Support time-based and event-based reminders. Support multi-stage escalation for overdue or critical work. Provide clear acknowledgement and completion lifecycle. Make processing idempotent and safe across retries/restarts. Preserve a complete audit trail. Support multiple branches and organizational reporting hierarchy. Keep business rules independent from notification delivery technology. Support Web/PWA first and native Android later without redesigning business logic. Keep the system simple for internal operational users. 2.2 Non-goals for v1.0 No consumer-style notification/marketing platform. No top-level Notifications business module. No dependency on a particular push provider. No native Android APK implementation in this specification phase. No automatic inference of criticality from vague business text; criticality must be explicitly defined. No hidden business decisions by the notification engine. 3. Terminology Term Definition Work Definition Reusable business definition of work, timing, evidence and escalation rules. Work Instance Concrete occurrence of a Work Definition assigned to a person/role/branch. Situation Business condition that may create work or change its urgency. Task Action required from an assignee to satisfy a Work Instance. Due Time Deadline by which required completion must occur. Reminder Stage Scheduled notification before or at the due point. Escalation Stage Scheduled/event-triggered alert to another authority when defined conditions are met. Acknowledgement Positive confirmation that the assignee has seen/accepted responsibility. Completion Business evidence that the work requirement is satisfied. Critical Work Work whose delay has a defined material operational consequence. Delivery Attempt One attempt to deliver a notification through a channel. Idempotency Key Stable key preventing duplicate processing of the same logical scheduled action. Notification Event Durable record representing a request to notify a recipient. Escalation Policy Ordered stages defining recipients and actions when work remains unresolved. 4. Reliability Model Reliability is a system property, not a single notification feature. Condition Required behavior Browser closed Server continues evaluating due work; PWA/web push can deliver where device/platform permits. Server process restarts Due work is reconstructed from durable database state; missed eligible stages are reconciled. Scheduler runs twice Idempotency prevents duplicate logical stage execution. Notification provider times out Attempt is recorded; retry policy applies. Push unavailable In-app state remains authoritative; escalation can continue independently. Employee ignores notification Acknowledgement/overdue policy may trigger escalation. Employee completes before reminder Pending reminder/escalation is cancelled or suppressed according to policy. Multiple branches Organization/branch isolation remains enforced. Timezone issue Business timezone comes from organization/location configuration. Duplicate work generation Work-instance idempotency key prevents duplicate instances. 5. Criticality Model Level Meaning Expected behavior NORMAL Routine operational work. Standard reminder/escalation policy. IMPORTANT Delay needs attention but is not immediately business-critical. Earlier reminder and defined escalation. CRITICAL Time-bound delay can materially disrupt operations. Mandatory escalation policy; stronger visibility; no silent failure. Criticality controls notification/escalation treatment configured for the work. It does not by itself decide a business outcome. 6. Criticality NORMAL / IMPORTANT / CRITICAL. Assignee type Employee, Role, Branch/Location responsibility, or supported future target. Assignee resolution How the actual responsible person is selected. Due rule Time-based, event-based, or hybrid. Evidence requirement What must be recorded/uploaded/confirmed to complete. Reminder stages Ordered stage list. Escalation stages Ordered stage list. Business timezone Resolved from organization/location settings. Grace period Optional defined tolerance after due time. Completion rule Conditions required for completion. Cancellation rule Conditions under which work is no longer applicable. Audit requirement State and escalation transitions are audited. 7. Timing Engine 7.1 Time-based rules Examples: every day at 7:00 AM, every Monday at 10:00 AM, or a defined offset before opening. Structured configuration must be used instead of free-text scheduling. 7.2 Event-based rules Examples: when a purchase schedule becomes due, when a branch opens, when a work instance is created, or when a previous stage completes/fails. 7.3 Hybrid rules A work instance may be created by an event and then use reminders/escalations relative to its due time. 7.4 Timezone Store timestamps consistently at the persistence boundary. Resolve business execution time from organization/location timezone configuration. Never use the server machine's local timezone as an implicit business rule. Record timezone context needed for audit. Test timezone boundaries even though the initial operating environment is India. 8. Reminder Stage Model Reminder stages are ordered and durable. Each stage must have a stable sequence/identity so it can be processed idempotently. Example Trigger Recipient Purpose R1 30 min before due Assignee Early preparation. R2 At due Assignee Due-time alert. R3 10 min overdue Assignee Immediate attention. These values are examples only. Actual defaults are configured when the relevant Work Definition is created. 9. Escalation Stage Model Stage Trigger Recipient Expected action E1 Overdue threshold Reporting person Alert and request intervention. E2 Further overdue Supervisor/Manager Escalate operational ownership. E3 Critical threshold Configured authority High-priority escalation. Recipients are resolved from current organizational data. Reporting relationships do not themselves grant authority; they are used only where the escalation policy says so. 10. Acknowledgement vs Completion State Meaning NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED Recipient has not confirmed receipt/acceptance. ACKNOWLEDGED Recipient confirmed they have seen/accepted the work. IN_PROGRESS Work has started where supported. COMPLETED Completion conditions satisfied. OVERDUE Due time passed without completion. ESCALATED An escalation stage executed. CANCELLED Work no longer applies under an authorized rule. Delivery is not acknowledgement. Provider delivery is not business completion. 11. Notification Channels Channel Initial role Authority In-app Always available when user accesses Kalki BOS. Authoritative application state. Web/PWA Push Primary phone channel for initial rollout. Delivery channel only. Android Push Future native app channel. Delivery channel only. WhatsApp/SMS Future optional escalation channels. Delivery channel only. The business engine creates durable notification events; channel adapters deliver them. 12. Core State Machine WORK_INSTANCE CREATED → ASSIGNED → ACKNOWLEDGED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED │ ├→ OVERDUE → ESCALATED → COMPLETED └→ CANCELLED Rules: • COMPLETED and CANCELLED are terminal unless an explicit correction workflow exists. • OVERDUE may transition to COMPLETED as a late completion. • ESCALATED does not mean completed. • State transitions are authorized and audited. • Reprocessing a stage never creates a second logical execution. 14. Scheduler Architecture The scheduler is server-side infrastructure. It must not depend on a browser, phone or open application tab. Persist work instance and timing requirements. Persist due/reminder/escalation stage identity. Claim eligible stages using database-safe locking/claim semantics. Create or claim an idempotent notification event. Execute the channel adapter. Record outcome and retry state. Update work/escalation state when required. Complete/release the stage transactionally where appropriate. 14.1 Restart recovery After restart, the scheduler queries durable eligible work based on current time and stage state. In-memory timers are never authoritative. 14.2 Missed-stage policy Each stage must declare whether a missed stage is executed immediately, skipped as obsolete, executed once as catch-up, or marked for manual review. The scheduler must not guess. 15. Idempotency and Concurrency Problem Control Same stage processed twice Unique logical stage execution key. Two workers claim same stage Atomic claim/row-locking strategy. Retry after timeout Idempotent provider request where supported; otherwise internal dedupe. Duplicate work generation Unique work-instance generation key. Duplicate escalation Unique work instance + escalation stage key. Concurrent completion/escalation Transactional state check before escalation commit. Repeated provider callback Provider event idempotency key / normalized dedupe. 16. Retry and Failure Handling Failure Required handling Transient channel failure Retry with controlled backoff. Permanent invalid recipient/device Mark delivery failed; surface appropriately. Provider outage Record failure; continue business escalation logic; retry later. Scheduler failure Restart recovery from database. Unknown processing result Do not assume success; reconcile/retry with idempotency. Repeated failure Create operational alert/incident according to severity. 17. Escalation Safety Do not escalate merely because a push notification was not confirmed delivered unless policy explicitly says so. Escalation normally follows business state: not completed, not acknowledged, or another explicit condition. Never duplicate the same escalation stage. If work completes before an escalation executes, suppress it unless policy explicitly requires historical notification. Resolve current recipients at execution time when hierarchy should be current, while preserving resolved recipients in history. 18. Security &amp; Authorization Organization isolation is mandatory. Branch/location scope is enforced server-side. Users see only authorized work/notifications. Critical escalations must respect recipient authorization. Notification payloads must avoid unnecessary sensitive information. Provider secrets/tokens never enter source control. Administrative configuration changes require permission and audit. Mandatory critical escalation channels cannot be silently disabled by ordinary user preferences. 19. User Experience Requirements Mobile-first notification experience. One-tap opening of relevant work. Clear title, urgency, due time and required action. Critical notifications visually distinct from routine reminders. Show overdue duration. Show acknowledgement and completion state. Do not force navigation through unrelated modules. Command Center surfaces overdue and critical work. Clear success/failure feedback; no silent actions. 20. It must not reproduce authoritative business scheduling logic locally. Correct: Kalki BOS Server → Work Engine → Escalation Engine → Notification Service → Android Push → Android App Incorrect: Android App → Local timer → Local business rules → Local escalation → independent data The Android client may cache data and support offline interaction where explicitly designed, but authoritative timing, business state and escalation remain server-side. 35. Architectural Freeze Notes Notification/reminder/escalation is a shared cross-system engine, not a top-level business module. Business timing and escalation are server-side. Notification channels are replaceable adapters. Web/PWA is the first phone delivery surface. Native Android is a later client, not a separate business engine. Critical work requires durable escalation and audit. The system recovers from process/server restarts. Idempotency is mandatory. No critical process silently fails because a browser tab is closed. Historical notification/escalation evidence is retained and auditable. 37. Never store only the next notification time without stage identity/history. Never equate provider success with business completion.
